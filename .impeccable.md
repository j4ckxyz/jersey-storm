## Design Context

### Users
Jersey and Guernsey islanders following Storm Austen (Thu 22 – Fri 23 Oct 2026), mostly on phones, often older, sometimes on low battery or poor signal during the storm. They check in repeatedly to answer: how bad is it right now, what's closed or cancelled, and what's coming next. The page is a single live tracker: summary, Jersey Met warnings and forecast, plus a merged feed of local news, gov.je and social posts.

### Brand Personality
Community and local: made in Jersey, for neighbours. Three words: **neighbourly, steady, local**. It should feel like a trusted islander keeping everyone posted. It should be warmer and have more personality than a corporate news app, while staying calm and credible.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Base direction: calm news app (chosen 2026-10-22).
- References: the polish of Apple Weather and Apple News (big numerals, soft surfaces, native-app feel).
- Anti-references: a generic SaaS dashboard (card-grid admin look, glassy chrome, stat-tile hero); tabloid or alarmist styling (flashing red, sirens, all-caps shouting).
- Theme: auto light/dark with a manual toggle (user's global preference).
- Stack: plain HTML/CSS/JS with no build step (no Tailwind in this codebase).

### Design Principles
1. **Severity comes from the data, not the chrome.** Warning colours are the only loud colour. The rest of the UI stays quiet so a red warning really reads as red.
2. **Glanceable first, detail on demand.** The current danger and the latest update are visible without scrolling on a phone. Impacts, full summary and multi-day forecast sit one tap away.
3. **Local voice.** Use island-specific language and landmarks, and make it feel human. Avoid generic tracker boilerplate.
4. **Readable for everyone.** WCAG AA contrast, ≥44px tap targets, body ≥16px, and it works one-handed on a small phone.
5. **Light on the network.** No heavy fonts or assets. It must stay usable on a weak storm-time connection.
